SYSTEM: DANDELION-SEED FIBER → COTTON-LIKE THREAD
MODE: fiber densification + cellulose reinforcement + micro-barb creation + high-tension spinning

────────────────────────────────────────
0. MATERIALS
────────────────────────────────────────
• 150–200 mature dandelion seed heads
• 30 g stems
• 30 g leaves
• 20 g young roots
• 200 mL water
• Latex: 1.0–1.5 mL (from fresh cuts)
• 20 mL 70% ethanol
• 1.0 g salt (NaCl)

────────────────────────────────────────
1. MAX-LENGTH FIBER COLLECTION
────────────────────────────────────────
Procedure:
1. Pull pappus tufts straight outward to preserve length.
2. Sieve and blow gently for 3–5 seconds.
Timing: 10 minutes.

Cotton effect:
• Longer fibers = cotton-like staple length.

────────────────────────────────────────
2. FIBER COLLAPSE PHASE (REMOVE HOLLOW STRUCTURE)
────────────────────────────────────────
Materials:
• 20 mL 70% ethanol
• 1.0 g salt

Procedure:
1. Dissolve salt in ethanol.
2. Mist fibers with 1–2 mL total.
3. Rest 15 minutes.

Cotton effect:
• Hollow fibers collapse → cotton-like density.
• Surface becomes slightly rough → better cohesion.

────────────────────────────────────────
3. HIGH-STRENGTH DANDELION CELLULOSE BINDER
────────────────────────────────────────
Materials:
• 30 g stems
• 30 g leaves
• 20 g roots
• 200 mL water
• 1.0–1.5 mL latex

Procedure:
A. Decoction
1. Chop stems/leaves/roots into 1–2 cm pieces.
2. Simmer in 200 mL water for 60 minutes.
3. Reduce to ~80 mL.
4. Mash thoroughly.
5. Strain → yields 40–50 mL gel.

B. Latex Integration
1. Collect 1.0–1.5 mL latex.
2. Dilute latex with 5 mL warm gel.
3. Mix back into main gel.

Binder use:
• 3–4 mL gel per 1 g pappus fibers.

Cotton effect:
• Dense cellulose gel simulates cotton fiber solidity.
• Latex adds natural “cling” similar to cotton micro‑barbs.

────────────────────────────────────────
4. MICRO-BARB CREATION (COTTON SURFACE SIMULATION)
────────────────────────────────────────
Procedure:
1. Spread 1 mL binder on glass.
2. Lay fibers in one direction.
3. Add a second layer perpendicular.
4. Drag a fine comb or glass slide lightly across fibers.
Timing: 5 minutes.

Cotton effect:
• Creates tiny frays and hooks → cotton-like grip.

────────────────────────────────────────
5. HIGH-DENSITY RIBBON FORMATION
────────────────────────────────────────
Procedure:
1. Add 2–3 mL binder across 10×10 cm area.
2. Lay fibers in 2–3 layers.
3. Press with flat tool to remove air pockets.
Timing: 10 minutes.

Cotton effect:
• Multi-layer ribbon = cotton-like bulk.

────────────────────────────────────────
6. PRESSURE-CHEM COMPRESSION (COTTON DENSITY)
────────────────────────────────────────
Materials:
• Parchment sheets
• Book press or clamp
• Heat source (55–65°C)

Procedure:
1. Sandwich ribbon between parchment.
2. Apply 15–20 kg distributed pressure.
3. Press 45 minutes.
4. Heat at 55–65°C for final 20 minutes.

Cotton effect:
• High pressure + heat = cotton-like fiber density.

────────────────────────────────────────
7. HIGH-TENSION SPINNING (COTTON STAPLE ALIGNMENT)
────────────────────────────────────────
Procedure:
1. Draft ribbon only 5–10% (minimal stretch).
2. Twist clockwise under 300–500 g tension.
3. Add micro-drops of binder during twist.
Timing: 5–8 minutes.

Cotton effect:
• High tension aligns fibers like cotton staples.
• Tight twist = cotton-like yarn strength.

────────────────────────────────────────
8. HARD TWIST SET (COTTON YARN FIRMNESS)
────────────────────────────────────────
Materials:
• Steam source
• Warm drying chamber

Procedure:
1. Hang thread under 300–600 g tension.
2. Steam 10–15 minutes at 80% humidity.
3. Dry at 55–65°C for 30 minutes.

Cotton effect:
• Firm, dense, cotton-like yarn body.

────────────────────────────────────────
9. FINAL COTTON-FINISH COAT (DANDELION GEL)
────────────────────────────────────────
Materials:
• 5 mL dandelion gel
• 1 mL latex
• 2 mL warm water

Procedure:
1. Mix gel + latex + water.
2. Brush thin coat onto thread.
3. Cure 24 hours.

Cotton effect:
• Smooth, matte, cotton-like finish.
• Increased abrasion resistance.

────────────────────────────────────────
RESULTING THREAD PROPERTIES
────────────────────────────────────────
• Density similar to cotton
• Stronger than basic dandelion thread (3–5×)
• Matte, soft, cotton-like surface
• High twist integrity
• Suitable for weaving, stitching, textile experiments

────────────────────────────────────────
